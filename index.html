Module 01: Core Education

Introduction to Artificial Intelligence

Course Content Curated by Dr. John Sands • Moraine Valley Community College

1. Understanding Artificial Intelligence

At its most fundamental level, Artificial Intelligence (AI) refers to the simulation of human intelligence processes by computer systems. These processes include learning (the acquisition of information and rules for using it), reasoning (using rules to reach approximate or definite conclusions), and self-correction.

Rather than operating as a single technology, AI functions as a vast umbrella term spanning machine learning, natural language processing, neural networks, computer vision, and expert systems.

🧠 Core AI Subfields: ML vs. Deep Learning

Machine Learning (ML)

A method of training algorithms so that they can learn how to make decisions or predictions. Instead of manually writing code with specific rules, the computer is fed large amounts of data and utilizes statistics to "learn" patterns.

Deep Learning (DL)

A specialized subset of machine learning inspired by the biological structure of the human brain. It uses multi-layered artificial neural networks to process unstructured input like images, high-dimensional arrays, and complex speech.

2. The Rise of Generative AI

In recent years, the conversational tech landscape has shifted toward Generative AI. Powered by massive neural net frameworks known as Transformer architectures (the "T" in ChatGPT), these models learn patterns from billions of parameters of historical text or code.

These models do not simply retrieve information; they calculate the statistical probability of the next optimal word or token in sequence, enabling them to draft code, write academic text, translate languages, and answer complex prompts with human-like prose.

3. Ethical AI & Fair Distribution

Because educational models have broad impacts on classroom learning and modern industry standards, ethics must remain central. These priorities include:

  • Data Bias Mitigation: Ensuring systems are trained on diverse and fair inputs to prevent perpetuating systemic inequalities.
  • Academic Integrity: Empowering students to use conversational AI as an active tutoring assistant, rather than a surrogate for personal learning.
  • Open Educational Resources (OER): Distributing curriculum materials under free-share licensing formats (like CC BY-NC) to maximize community access while preventing corporate exploitation of student resources.
💡
Dr. Sands' Classroom Tip: "Think of Artificial Intelligence as an intellectual amplifier. It can speed up your software development and data structuring immensely, but you must remain the master architect who validates the outputs for precision, logical soundness, and security."
